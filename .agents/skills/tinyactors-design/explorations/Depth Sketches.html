Exploration · depth

Perspective, depth and layers

Twelve sketches, from flat layering that fits the current drafting style to real perspective. Hover or click each one; most also run on their own.

Group A

Layers without perspective

Depth from paper, not from light. These fit inside the existing system with no new rendering techniques.

1

Vellum overlays

Live state on a sheet of tracing paper over the structure. Here the base is the buffer and the sheet carries the heads and the unread span. Hover to lift the sheet.

2

Registration offsets

Earlier generations sit behind the current sheet, offset a few pixels with crop marks, like an actor restarted twice. Hover to fan them out.

3

Paper levels

Four fixed levels to replace ad-hoc shadows. Each step up is lighter and has a firmer edge; only the top level casts a shadow.

0 deskgrid, dashed edge
1 sheetsurface-1, hairline
2 overlayvellum, see-through
3 loupeink edge, shadow-pop
Group B

Depth as meaning

Distance stands for something real: latency, attention or interaction.

4

Memory hierarchy as distance

Each tier steps back in size and contrast as latency grows. A load probe travels down to where the data lives and back, timed on a log scale.

5

Focus and depth of field

The actor that matters stays sharp and lifts slightly; the rest go soft. Focus moves to whichever actor just handled a message.

6

Lift on interaction

Hover a block: it rises one paper level, leaves its footprint behind, and its traces stretch to follow.

Group C

Real perspective

Actual 3D projection. Strongest for teaching and the website; mostly too much for the inspector.

7

Exploded axonometric

struct actor_hdr split into header, payload and padding, stacked at an axonometric angle. Click to collapse and explode.

8

Mailbox as a stack

Messages recede into depth by age. New ones arrive at the front; the oldest at the back is consumed and drops away.

9

Tilted board

The architecture drawing as a board seen at an angle, blocks extruded like chips. Hover to flatten it back to the plan view.

10

Orbitable model

The same struct as solid blocks, one per field, length by bytes. Drag to orbit. A production version would use a real 3D viewer with GLB export.

drag
Group D

Motion and depth

Depth that only exists while something moves.

11

Grid parallax

The construction grid scrolls at half speed behind the sheets, so content reads as floating above the drafting table. Website only.

Spawn

An actor is a statechart with a mailbox.

ta_actor *a = ta_spawn(sys, &chart);

Send

Messages queue in a lock-free ring.

ta_send(a, "PAY", &evt);

Snapshot

State is a value you can inspect and replay.

ta_state s = ta_snapshot(a);

Supervise

Crashes restart from the last good state.

ta_supervise(sys, a, TA_RESTART);
12

Diving into a block

Click ingest to zoom into its components; the system view falls back behind as context. Use the path to come back up.

Notes

Where each one fits

  • Inspector: 3 everywhere, 1 and 5 for live state and focus, 6 on the architecture canvas, 12 for navigating system → container → component.
  • Docs and website: 4, 7 and 8 for teaching memory; 9, 10 and 11 for hero moments only.
  • Reduced motion: every sketch keeps its final state; only the transitions are dropped.
